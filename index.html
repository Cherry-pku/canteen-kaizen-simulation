K1 · Online pre-order. The selected share orders before arrival, while remaining customers still order at the counter. Each dish steams for eight minutes; lead time shifts some or all of that work before arrival.
K2 · Demand-based pre-preparation. Reallocate 40 prepared portions across eight dishes to reduce shortages and emergency replenishment without increasing total starting stock.
K3 · Staff coordination + 5S. Staff 1 prepares, loads, and unloads dishes. Staff 2 handles orders, rice/vegetable/drink assembly, and pickup calls. Place drinks and frequent-use items closer to assembly.
K4 · Ordering improvement. Use numbered dishes and clear sold-out indicators. Shorter counter ordering can free Staff 2 sooner for pickup assembly.

Model notes & editable assumptions

Results are estimates for a class project, not measured canteen performance.

discrete-event method
Given by your group: 2 staff; current sequence of 20 seconds to order, 8 minutes steaming, and 35 seconds to unload, assemble, and hand over the meal; around 20 steamer spaces. The no-queue process time is 8 minutes 55 seconds.
Temporary assumptions: one dish per student; the 35-second finishing step is split into 10 seconds for Staff 1 to unload and 25 seconds for Staff 2 to assemble and hand over. Loading time is not separately measured and is set to zero. K3 is assumed to save 10 seconds of assembly time; K4 reduces counter ordering from 20 to 15 seconds. A 30-second delay per replenishment event is a placeholder for K2. Staff 2 cannot order and assemble simultaneously. Replace these assumptions with observations before treating predicted queues as real.
